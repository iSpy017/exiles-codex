# League Start Framework (from "The Only POE2 League Start Guide you NEED")

Source: video transcript supplied by David, 2026-11-17 (12-page PDF, read in full). Creator name not in the transcript. Strategy opinion, not game data → treat as tier 3/5: useful framing, no mechanics claims accepted without verification.
Version: written pre-1.0 (creator refers to an upcoming league with Trial of Chaos and "citadels"). Volatility: may-change.

## Core idea
- Pick your effort level first (casual / semi-sweat / super-sweat). Everyone follows the same road — campaign → maps → T15 → farming → endgame build — only the speed differs.
- Pick the content you want to farm second, then the build that can do it. The starter is a "rental car": its job is to get you to money, not to be the final build.
- The real race is not "character creation to maps" but "cheapest route to financial freedom". A starter that reaches maps 30 min later but converts straight into a strong T15 farmer can earn more, faster.

## Effort tiers (creator's definitions)
- Casual: plays for fun, a few hours a day; same checklist over 1–3 weeks.
- Semi-sweat: researches build and farm in advance, optimises limited time, not 18-hour days.
- Super-sweat: has practised the campaign, knows layouts, gem cuts, vendor checks and passive order; aims to clear campaign before the economy forms. Creator advises most players against it.

## Content → build requirements (creator's view)
- Ritual: AoE + defence; handle monsters spawning around you in a confined space.
- Expedition: AoE + damage, with enough survivability for escalating modifiers.
- Citadels: movement, clear speed, boss damage.
- Trial of Chaos: survivability + bossing, plus ways to ignore danger-scaling modifiers.
- Notes: "Citadels" and the state of Trial of Chaos in 1.0 are unverified → [[endgame/atlas]], [[endgame/trial-of-chaos]].

## The 5-divine T15 test
- Can the build farm T15 with ≤5 div of gear? Know the first 3–5 upgrades (weapon, +skill levels, spirit, movement speed, defences, resists) before you earn the currency. If the build's key pieces cost more than 5 div, pivot or use a different starter.

## Map progression checkpoints
- Campaign: don't over-invest in gear you'll replace. Enough damage, defence, resists, movement speed — keep moving.
- T1–~T6: progression only. Fix resists, damage, defence, movement; don't settle into comfy low-tier farming.
- ~T7: start reading the ground — higher item level means better mod tiers and crafting bases start to matter. Ask "can someone else use this?", not just "can I use this?". Learn the bases of the launch meta (e.g. spears if Huntress is popular, bows, quarterstaffs, +skill-level weapons for casters) plus universal bases: movement-speed boots, jewellery, spirit, defence bases.
  - Game-fact lead to verify: item-level thresholds for mod tiers → [[items/item-level]], [[items/affix-tiers]] (batch 7). Creator gives no numbers.
- Valuable-item checklist (4 questions): desirable base? useful item level? already 1–2 premium mods? cheap to improve? An unfinished item one cheap craft from done can be worth more than currency. But don't spend 20 ex on a 3 ex item early.
- ~T11+: take crafting seriously.
- Any checkpoint: if you can keep progressing efficiently, keep going; if not, name the bottleneck (damage, defence, resists, sustain) and fix it.
- T15 "job done" gate: clears repeatedly, sustains waystones, kills bosses fast, completes the chosen mechanic, doesn't keep dying. Then the mindset switches from progression to economics.

## Making money
- Farm what players behind you need: waystones, tablets, weapons, crafting bases, uniques, progression items.
- Don't marry your farm: the market keeps telling you what's valuable; re-evaluate (casual weekly, sweat daily, super-sweat hourly) and pivot if a new mechanic's rewards spike.
- Creator's own plan: alch-and-go mapping, push the atlas, farm tablets, chase citadel rewards.

## Investment cut-off
- Ask whether the next divine makes more money. 5 div that doubles clear speed: yes. 20 div for 10% more damage when you already one-shot everything: no. Once the starter does its job, extra spending must justify itself; save for the real build.

## Full checklist (paraphrased)
1. How hard are you sweating? 2. What do you want to accomplish / farm? 3. What does that content need? 4. Which character gets you there? 5. Can it reach farmable T15s cheaply? 6. What are the first critical upgrades? 7. Campaign: move forward. 8. Early maps: fix problems, push. 9. T7: watch for valuable drops; learn bases vs crafts. 10. T11+: craft. 11. T15: prove you can farm it. 12. Execute, supply players behind you, watch the economy, pivot, stop investing when the starter's done. 13. Build the "monster".
